Choose by learning need

Which RPSGT study book should you start with?

Broad RPSGT foundation

Start with Fundamentals of Sleep Technology when you want one wide reference covering physiology, recording, scoring context, treatment, pediatrics, and sleep-center operations.

Instrumentation and troubleshooting

Use Polysomnography for the Sleep Technologist when filters, sensors, electronics, signal acquisition, artifact, gas exchange, or technical procedures are your weakest areas.

Pediatric sleep

Use Pediatric Sleep Pearls or A Clinical Guide to Pediatric Sleep when you need stronger pediatric development, PSG context, clinical presentation, or family-centered reasoning.

Study hierarchy

Books support study. Current standards still control.

Textbooks are valuable because they explain concepts in depth, but they age. When a detail is version-sensitive, use the current authoritative source.

  • AASM Scoring Manual: current scoring rules, event definitions, technical specifications, and terminology.
  • BRPT: current RPSGT candidate information, eligibility requirements, examination policies, and exam blueprint.
  • AAST and other current professional guidance: technical practice guidance where applicable.
  • Employer policy and clinical leadership: facility-specific procedures and escalation requirements.

Frequently asked questions

RPSGT book and study questions

Do I need to buy every RPSGT exam prep book?

No. Start with the current blueprint and your weakest domains, then choose the book that fills those gaps. The Guild’s RPSGT study webapp remains free.

Which book is strongest for instrumentation and troubleshooting?

Polysomnography for the Sleep Technologist is especially useful for electronics, sensors, filters, data acquisition, artifacts, monitoring, and technical procedures.

Which books help with pediatric sleep review?

Pediatric Sleep Pearls and A Clinical Guide to Pediatric Sleep support pediatric clinical reasoning, PSG context, development, and pediatric sleep-disorder review.

Do these books replace the AASM Scoring Manual?

No. Use textbooks to understand concepts and context. Use the current AASM Scoring Manual and other current official sources for version-sensitive rules and requirements.
